## My Roofer Wants to Put New Shingles Over the Old Ones. Is That Okay?

Getting a quote for a roof 'overlay' can be tempting. It's almost always cheaper than a full tear-off, but it's one of those situations where saving money now can cost you a lot more down the road.

First, there's the building code to consider. In most LA-area jurisdictions, you are not allowed to have more than two layers of roofing material on your home. If you already have more than one, a tear-off isn't just a good idea—it's the law. An overlay would fail inspection.

Second, an overlay conceals the true condition of your roof deck. You're simply covering up any potential soft spots, rot, or water damage in the underlying wood sheathing. This can lead to serious structural problems and leaks that a new layer of shingles won't stop.

Third, performance is compromised. A new roof performs best when it's installed on a clean, flat surface. An overlay can result in a lumpy appearance and can even shorten the lifespan of your new shingles, potentially voiding the manufacturer's warranty.

For a durable, watertight, and fully warrantied roof, a complete tear-off is the professional standard. If you want a roof done right, with no shortcuts, call YOR Construction for a proper evaluation.

## The Biggest Mistakes to Avoid With Your ADU Build

Building an ADU is an exciting investment, but a few common missteps can quickly lead to budget overruns and frustrating delays. Knowing what to watch out for from the very beginning can make all the difference.

Finally, don't start any work before you have approved permits in hand. It's tempting to get a head start on demolition or site prep, but doing so can create massive problems. ## Is It Better to Repair or Replace My Leaky Roof?

When you see a water stain on your ceiling, it's easy to panic. The big question is whether you need a simple repair or a full-blown replacement. The right answer depends on a few key factors.

First, how widespread is the problem? If you have a single, isolated leak on a relatively young roof, a professional repair is often the most sensible and cost-effective solution. However, if you're experiencing multiple leaks in different areas, it could be a sign that the entire roofing system is starting to fail. Second, consider the age of your roof. If it's approaching the end of its typical lifespan, investing in repeated repairs might be throwing good money after bad. A full replacement is a bigger upfront investment, but it provides long-term peace of mind. Finally, you can't see everything from the ground. There could be hidden damage to the substrate or wood decking underneath that needs to be addressed.

Ultimately, the only way to make an informed decision is with a thorough professional inspection. A qualified roofer can identify the true source and extent of the damage. For an honest evaluation and a clear estimate in the Los Angeles County area, call YOR Construction & Investments, Inc. today.

## How Does a Seismic Retrofit Actually Work?

How Does a Seismic Retrofit Actually Work?

## What are the Most Common (and Costly) Seismic Retrofitting Mistakes?

When it comes to seismic retrofitting, the details make all the difference. Doing it wrong can not only waste your investment but also fail to protect your building when you need it most. As experts in Southern California construction, we've seen a few common and costly mistakes that are easily avoided with the right approach.

One of the biggest errors is failing to create a "continuous load path." Think of it like a chain. A retrofit might add strong foundation bolts (one strong link), but if those bolts aren't connected to properly braced walls, and the walls aren't properly tied to the floors and roof, the chain is broken. The entire system has to work together to safely transfer earthquake forces to the ground. An incomplete system is a failed system.

Another frequent oversight is ignoring non-structural hazards. Many people focus only on the building's frame. But in an earthquake, heavy elements like ceilings, light fixtures, and mechanical equipment can fall, causing major damage and potential injury, even if the building itself remains standing. A thorough retrofit addresses these risks, too.

Finally, a one-size-fits-all approach can be dangerous. Adding very stiff elements to one part of a building without considering how it affects the rest of the structure can create new problems, like a "soft story" that takes on a disproportionate amount of force. A professional retrofit analyzes the entire building to ensure the solution works as a whole.
