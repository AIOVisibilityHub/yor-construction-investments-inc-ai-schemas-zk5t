How long does a typical home addition take from first idea to move‑in?

Most home additions take several months from first idea to the day you fully use the space. The total timeline includes planning and design, permits, and construction. Key factors: - Design and planning: 4–12+ weeks (longer for complex additions) - Permitting: 4–12+ weeks depending on city workload - Construction: 8–24+ weeks based on size and complexity - Inspection and punch‑list: 2–4 weeks Ask your contractor for a written timeline broken into phases and dependencies (e.g., permit approval before start). In Los Angeles, most home additions require building, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical permits, and sometimes zoning or planning review. Key factors: - Zoning rules: setbacks, height limits, floor‑area ratio - Structural and seismic requirements for new construction - Energy, insulation, and ventilation standards (Title 24) - Possible plan checks from building, fire, and sometimes public works Before design gets too far, ask your contractor to confirm zoning feasibility and outline which departments must review the plans. Clarify in writing who will prepare drawings, fill out forms, pay fees upfront, and track corrections. Work with a team already familiar with LA‑area permitting to avoid costly back‑and‑forth.

Will a home addition increase my property taxes in Los Angeles County, and by how much?

Yes, adding new square footage usually increases your property tax because the new construction is reassessed separately from your existing home. The exact amount depends on the value the assessor assigns to the addition and the local tax rate. In LA County, the assessor reviews building permits and may add the market value of the new work to your current assessed value. It’s not a full reassessment of the entire property in most cases, but it will raise your annual bill. Key factors: - Size and type of addition (basic room vs. high‑end suite) - Construction cost and resulting market value - Current assessed value and local tax rate - Possible exemptions for certain improvements Before you commit, ask your contractor for a realistic finished value increase estimate and then call the county assessor’s office for guidance. Include a property tax bump in your long‑term affordability calculations so the project doesn’t strain your budget after it’s built.
